\section{Invariant Preservation}
\label{sec:formal}

This section states the preservation results that the experiment is designed to test.
The propositions are stated as falsifiable predictions: each names the invariant, the
window or crash point it concerns, and the measurement that can refute it. Proof
sketches are given for the closure results; concrete counterexample traces are given for
the violations. The pen-and-paper argument is complemented by a bounded TLA+
\cite{lamport1994} model that was machine-checked with TLC at the bounds stated in
Section~\ref{sec:formal:tlc}; the model is a bounded witness of the crash-point
semantics, not an unbounded proof.

\subsection{Closure conditions}
\label{sec:formal:closure}

Lemma~\ref{lem:windows} leaves exactly two ways to close the windows of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:w1}--\eqref{eq:w2}: make the effect idempotent, or make the effect and
the progress one atomic commit. Lemma~\ref{lem:closure} records which mechanism property
closes which window.

\begin{lemma}[Closure]
\label{lem:closure}
A mechanism preserves I3 against a schedule in $W_1$ if and only if its effect is
idempotent under the business key $(\mathit{event\_id},\mathit{leg\_no})$ or its effect
and progress are one atomic commit. A mechanism preserves I4 against a schedule in $W_2$
if and only if its effect and progress are one atomic commit; de-duplication is
irrelevant to $W_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proofsketch}
($W_1$, $\Leftarrow$) If the effect is idempotent, re-executing the batch after a
redelivery is a no-op on the sink; if effect and progress are atomic, a durable effect
implies durable progress, so no redelivery occurs. ($W_1$, $\Rightarrow$) If neither
holds, take a crash between the two commits (trace X1): the effect is durable, progress
is lost, the batch is redelivered, and the plain insert applies the effect twice.
($W_2$) In a $W_2$ schedule progress is durable and the effect is absent; no redelivery
occurs, so there is nothing for de-duplication to suppress and only atomicity prevents
the state (trace X3). \hfill$\square$
\end{proofsketch}

Lemma~\ref{lem:closure} classifies every mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}.
M0 and M1x have neither property and fail $W_1$; M3 has de-duplication and fails $W_2$
under the early probe; M4 and M2 have atomicity and fail neither, except for the
concurrent-writer exposure of M4 (crash point $C_7$), which is not a window between
commits but a second writer. The combinations add the missing property to the base
mechanism. The classification is the model's answer to RQ1 and is quantified --- per
failure class and per mechanism --- by the experiment; the rows of
Table~\ref{tab:crashverdicts} are the predictions that the measurements can refute.

\subsection{Propositions}
\label{sec:formal:props}

\begin{proposition}[EOS boundary]
\label{prop:p1}
Kafka's atomic commit covers exactly the transitions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:eos}; for every
mechanism except M1-kafka the ledger commit is outside it, so under $W_1$ the internal
publication stream and the external ledger can both be ``correct'' on their own terms
while diverging from each other.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
By construction of the boundary: the transaction includes Kafka appends and offsets, and
the database commit is a separate site (Corollary~\ref{cor:eosgap}). The divergence is
realised by trace X4: an aborted transaction discards the stream records while the
database rows persist, and the retry appends a second ledger row but a single stream
record. Refuted if M1x shows no divergence while its transaction demonstrably aborts.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[$W_1$ closure]
\label{prop:p2}
Under strict ordering and crash-recovery faults, a mechanism preserves I3 in every
schedule of $W_1$ if and only if it is idempotent under the business key or commits
effect and progress atomically.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
Lemma~\ref{lem:closure} applied to the $W_1$ schedules; the counterexample is trace X1
and the positive cases are traces X2 and X5. The test is the deterministic $C_4$
injection (class $K_3$) with M0 as the negative control.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[$W_2$ closure]
\label{prop:p3}
A mechanism preserves I4 against the early-ordering probe if and only if its effect and
progress are one atomic commit; de-duplication cannot restore a posting that was never
written.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
Trace X3: progress is durable, the effect is absent, and the batch is never redelivered,
so the posting is missing irrespective of any unique index. Tested by the early-ordering
probe at class $K_4$ across all mechanisms; predicted to fail for every mechanism whose
progress is a separate commit, including M3 and M2.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[M0 negative control]
\label{prop:p4}
M0 yields duplicates (I3 violations) under the deterministic $W_1$ injection and under
duplicate redelivery, because it has neither de-duplication nor atomic progress.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}, failure direction. If M0 is clean under an attested $C_4$
injection the harness is broken, so M0 is the campaign's negative control (failure is
the expected observation, not an anomaly).
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[M1-kafka positive control]
\label{prop:p5}
M1-kafka preserves I1--I4 in every executed cell: both duties are inside one Kafka
transaction \cite{kip98}, and the publication stream is its own ledger.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
A replay of an uncommitted transaction produces no durable record, and a committed
transaction carries its offsets atomically; producer fencing excludes a zombie. Any
violation in M1-kafka is a harness or configuration defect and fails the positive-control
gate.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[M1x fails where the gap opens]
\label{prop:p6}
M1x yields duplicates (I3) in the $W_1$ crash classes and the corresponding fault
conditions, while its publication stream remains exactly-once; it has no $W_2$ exposure
under strict ordering.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
The ledger rows are committed outside the transaction, while the stream records and the
offsets are inside it; a crash between the database commit and the transaction commit
(trace X4) duplicates the ledger and discards the stream record of the aborted
transaction. Tested by the $C_4/C_{10}$ injections and by the ``broker killed
mid-produce'' fault.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[M2 sufficiency]
\label{prop:p7}
M2 preserves I1--I4 in all executed cells: the inbox claim makes redelivery a no-op, the
database transaction makes the effect atomic, and the outbox makes publication
recoverable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
Each $W_1$ schedule is absorbed by the claim (trace X2 with a table instead of an
index); $W_2$ is unreachable under strict ordering; the relay window concerns only
publication (trace X7). M2's cost is a separate, measured prediction.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[M3: duplicates closed, losses open]
\label{prop:p8}
M3 preserves I1--I4 in the $W_1$ crash classes and yields no duplicate in any modelled
condition, but loses postings under the early-ordering probe.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
The unique index absorbs every replay ($W_1$, trace X2). Under the probe, progress is
durable before the effect, no redelivery occurs, and the index has no row to conflict
with ($W_2$, trace X3). This is the formal content of H3.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[M4: atomicity without de-duplication]
\label{prop:p9}
M4 preserves I1--I4 in the $W_1$ and $W_2$ crash classes because effect and progress are
one database transaction, but it is exposed to a concurrent writer (crash point $C_7$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
A durable transaction advances the stored offset atomically, so a crash can only leave
both or neither durable ($W_1$/$W_2$ unreachable, trace X5). A paused owner that resumes
after reassignment writes a second copy with no constraint to stop it (trace X6). This is
why the combination M4+M3 exists.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[Combinations]
\label{prop:p10}
M2+M3, M4+M3 and M1x+M3 each close exactly the exposure missing from their base
mechanism and introduce no new durable duty.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
Superposition of Propositions~\ref{prop:p7}--\ref{prop:p9}: the added component is a
constraint (a unique index), not a protocol step, so it cannot open a window. Predicted
clean in every executed cell; the cost differences are measured.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[Balance checks are blind to duplication]
\label{prop:p11}
There are schedules in which I3 is violated while I1 holds: a duplicated balanced entry
sums to zero. Consequently a practitioner who checks only that every entry balances will
not detect the duplicates produced by the EOS gap.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
A second copy of a balanced entry is itself balanced; the per-entry signed sum remains
zero and the legs are structurally well-formed. Only the count of entries per business
event (I3) changes. The experiment counts runs in which I3 is violated while I1 is not;
the checker's mutation test certifies that a duplicated row is detectable.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{proposition}[No dominance]
\label{prop:p12}
Under the model no single mechanism is simultaneously complete on correctness and
best on cost: M3 is the cheapest complete ledger mechanism under strict ordering but is
blind to $W_2$; M4 closes both windows without an index but is open to $C_7$ and does not
preserve publication exactly-once; M1x+M3 is required for ledger integrity plus
Kafka-internal exactly-once publication and pays the transaction cost; M2 buys
recoverable publication at the highest write cost.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proofsketch}
Lemma~\ref{lem:closure} and the distinctness matrix of Table~\ref{tab:distinct}: each
mechanism closes a different subset, and the properties that close correctness
(dedup, atomicity) are not the properties that reduce cost. H5 is supported if the
measured performance ordering shows at least one of these trade-offs; it is refuted if
some mechanism is both clean in every correctness cell and non-inferior in throughput
and tail latency.
\end{proofsketch}

\subsection{Bounded machine-checked model}
\label{sec:formal:tlc}

The crash-point semantics of Section~\ref{sec:model} were encoded in a small TLA+
\cite{lamport1994} model whose state consists of per-$(\mathit{event},\mathit{leg})$
posting multiplicities, the durable progress, the publication records, and a crash flag.
Four booleans parameterise the mechanism family: de-duplication, atomicity of effect and
progress, atomicity of publication and progress (the Kafka transaction), and the
early-ordering probe. The model is deliberately small --- three events, batches of two,
one partition --- and the bounds were fixed before any check.

Table~\ref{tab:tlc} reports the checked outcome. All eight configurations matched the
expectation recorded before checking: the naive and transaction-only configurations
violate the uniqueness invariant (I3) --- a duplicate posting is reachable --- while the
de-duplicating and atomic configurations violate none, and both early-ordering probes
violate completeness (I4) --- a posting is lost while the unique index is present. The
two probes are the machine-checked form of Proposition~\ref{prop:p3}: de-duplication
does not preserve completeness.

Three qualifications bound the claim. First, the model is a \emph{bounded witness}:
it explores a finite state space, so it demonstrates that the two windows are reachable
and that the closure conditions hold for the modelled protocols at those bounds; it is
not an unbounded proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}. Second, the model abstracts away
multi-partition scheduling, rebalancing, the zombie writer and the relay path
(Section~\ref{sec:model:assumptions}); those cells rest on the pen-and-paper argument and
on the deterministic injections of Section~\ref{sec:method}. Third, the encoding is a
model of the protocols, not of the Java implementation: it can refute an incorrect
closure claim, but it cannot establish that the artifact implements the modelled protocol
--- that is the role of the independent oracle and the harness-validation gates. The
model, the eight configurations, the TLC invocation and the logs are archived with the
artifact, and the bounded results are reported as such in the paper.
